Why corporates are going video

Video is how buyers decide whether they trust you, before they've spent a rupee of your time.

Buyers research a company on video before they ever take a call. A founder's face on screen does something a case study PDF can't. It lets a prospect decide whether they trust you before they've spent a rupee of your time. Investors do the same thing before a first meeting. Candidates do it before applying. Video has become the default way people vet a company, not an optional extra.

The shift isn't just about visibility. It's about compounding. A blog post gets read once. A LinkedIn post scrolls past in a day. A video, cut well, keeps earning views on YouTube for years, keeps getting reshared as a Short, and keeps showing up in someone's feed months after it was shot. One good day of filming keeps working long after the invoice is paid.

What each platform actually does for you

Three platforms, three different jobs.

YouTube
Depth & authority

This is where depth lives. Long-form video is where a founder gets to actually explain their thinking, not just perform a soundbite. It's also the platform with the longest shelf life. A good video ranks on search and keeps bringing in views a year later. This is the channel for building real authority, not just visibility.

Instagram
Reach & recall

This is where reach and recall live. Shorts get discovered by people who've never heard of you, and repetition here is what makes a founder's face and voice familiar before a prospect ever gets on a call. It's volume-driven. The more consistently you show up, the more the algorithm keeps showing you to new people.

LinkedIn
Buyers & conversion

This is where the actual buyers are. LinkedIn video reaches the people already in your industry, already in a buying role, already primed to take your content seriously because it showed up in a professional context. It converts differently from Instagram: fewer views, but views from the right people.

Run together, the three platforms do different jobs. LinkedIn builds credibility with buyers, Instagram builds broad recall, YouTube builds depth and search presence. Most founders only ever do one of these well. The point of running all three off the same footage is that none of them get neglected.
How we run it, start to finish

Five stages, one team, one monthly rhythm.

01

Content calendar

Every month starts with a short call. We map out what you want to say, what's happening in your business worth talking about, and which platform each idea suits best. You leave the call with a plan, not a blank page.

02

Scripting

We turn that plan into a shot list and talking points for each piece. Enough structure that you're not improvising cold, loose enough that it still sounds like you and not a script being read. No memorizing lines.

03

Production

We shoot on-site with you, across two days a month. You talk, we direct and capture. Nothing to set up, nothing to operate, nothing to learn.

04

Edit

Every piece gets cut for the platform it's going to. Long-form for YouTube, vertical cuts with captions for Shorts and Reels, native edits for LinkedIn. Each one is built for how people actually watch on that platform, not the same file resized three ways.

05

Scheduling

We post on your handles, on an agreed cadence, so content goes out consistently through the month instead of arriving in a dump right after the shoot.
